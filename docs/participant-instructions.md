# Participant-facing instructions

Every screen a participant reads in the **Faces Go/No-Go**, **PILT (card
choosing)**, **Vigour** and **Reversal** games.

This was **captured from running sessions**, not transcribed from source — each
task was played through in a browser and every screen recorded as rendered. Text
in the code is wrapped in conditionals (touch vs keyboard, session, valence), so
reading it off the source risks reporting wording no participant ever sees.

Captured on a tablet (touch) and on desktop (keyboard) so both variants appear
below. The study runs on an Android tablet, so **the touch wording is the live
one**; keyboard wording is noted where it differs.

Session shown: `wk0`, the standard non-screening session.

---

## Shared screens

### Orientation (every touch task, before anything else)

> For this game, please hold your tablet in **landscape (on its side)** mode.
>
> `[Got it]`

Says *phone* on a phone, and *portrait (upright)* for Vigour. Not shown on desktop.

### Slow-response warnings (any task, only if triggered)

Shown after enough missed response deadlines:

> You seem to be taking too long to respond on the games.
>
> Please try to respond more quickly. Also, please keep your attention on the game window, and don't use other tabs or windows.
>
> `[Continue]`

And, separately:

> You might be taking a little too long to make your choices.
>
> We're interested in your quick judgments, so please try to respond a little faster—even if it feels a bit less precise.
>
> `[Continue]`

Neither ends the session. **Nothing in the battery terminates a session.**

## 1. Faces Go/No-Go

### Instructions

**1.**
> **THE PEOPLE GAME**
>
> In this game you will see pictures of people, one at a time.
>
> For each person, you decide whether to tap or not.

**2.**
> Each picture stays on screen for a moment.
>
> **To tap:** touch the picture.
> **To not tap:** just wait, and the picture will go away on its own.
>
> Choose quickly - you do not have long.

*Keyboard: "**To tap:** press the space bar."*

**3.**
> With some people, there is **money to win**.
>
> Get it right and you win **£1**.  🪙
> Get it wrong and you win only **1p**.  🪙
>
> With others, there is **money to lose**.
>
> Get it right and you lose only **1p**.  🪙
> Get it wrong and you lose **£1**.  🪙

*Coin images shown inline: £1, 1p, broken 1p, broken £1.*

**4.**
> The same people come back again and again.
>
> Some are best tapped. Others are best left alone. You cannot tell by looking - you have to find out by trying.
>
> Watch what happens each time, and try to remember what works for each person.

**5.**
> Even when you make the better choice, you may sometimes get only a penny, or occasionally break a £1 coin.
>
> First, let's try a few.

### Practice

Four single-face stages, each repeating until performance criterion:

> **Let's try tapping.** (1 of 4)
> Tap this person's picture and see what happens.
> When you get it right, you win **£1**.

> **Now let's try waiting.** (2 of 4)
> This time, do not tap. Just wait.
> When you get it right, you win **£1**.

> **Sometimes there is money to lose.** (3 of 4)
> Here, tapping is the better choice.
> Get it right and you lose only **1p**. Get it wrong and you lose **£1**.

> **One more.** (4 of 4)
> Here, waiting is the better choice.
> Get it right and you lose only **1p**. Get it wrong and you lose **£1**.

Then all four interleaved, in blocks of 8, until each face has 2 correct:

> **Now all four together.**
>
> You will see the same four people, mixed up.
>
> Each one works the same way as it did just now.

### Comprehension check

> Nicely done.
>
> Before you start, a few quick questions.
>
> You need all of them right to go on.

Preamble: *Is each sentence true or false?*

| # | Statement | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | For each person, I choose whether to tap or not. | True |
| 2 | A broken coin means I have lost money. | True |
| 3 | The same people come back, so I can learn what works for each one. | True |

On failure: *"Not quite. Have another go."* — loops until all correct.

The task begins immediately after; there is no separate "ready" screen.

## 2. PILT (card choosing)

### Instructions

**1.**
> **THE CARD CHOOSING GAME**
>
> In this game you will flip cards to collect the coins behind them.
>
> Some cards are luckier than others. Your goal is to collect as much game money as possible and avoid losing it.
>
> At the end of this session, you will be paid a bonus based on the sum of coins you collected.

**2.**
> On each turn of this game, you will see two cards. You have **four seconds** to flip one of the two cards.
>
> This will reveal the coin you collect: either 1 pound, 50 pence, or 1 penny.

*"Four seconds" is derived from the configured response deadline, so it always matches the task.*

**3.**
> When you flip a card, you might see broken coins like these:  🪙🪙🪙
>
> This means you lose that amount of game coins.

**4.**
> Sometimes, losing coins cannot be avoided. Your goal then is to lose as little money as possible.
>
> To cover these losses, you will start the game with **£100** in game coins.

**5.**
> Some cards are better than others, and through trial and error, you can learn which ones are best.
>
> However, even the best cards may sometimes give only a penny or occasionally break a one-pound coin.

### Practice

> Let's practice collecting coins. On the next screen, choose cards to collect as much money as you can.
>
> One of the picture cards has mostly £1 coins behind it, while the other has mostly broken £1 coins behind it.
>
> When you're ready, tap the button below to begin.  `[I'm ready]`

*Keyboard: "place your fingers comfortably on the left and right arrow keys… Press down both left and right arrow keys at the same time to begin."*

After the practice round:

> Altogether on this round, you've collected coins worth £X.XX.
>
> Tap the button below to continue.  `[Continue]`

*Keyboard: "Press the right arrow to continue."*

### Comprehension check

> Before you start playing, you'll answer a few questions about the instructions you just read.
>
> You must answer all questions correctly to begin the game.
>
> If not, you can review the instructions and try again.

Preamble: *For each statement, please indicate whether it is true or false:*

| # | Statement | Correct |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Some cards are better than others, but even the best cards might only give a penny or break a £1 coin. | True |
| 2 | If I find a broken coin, that means I lose that amount. | True |
| 3 | My goal is to collect as much game coins as I can and avoid losing them. | True |

On failure, the wrong answers are shown with an explanation, then the quiz repeats.

### Starting, and between rounds

> Great! Let's start playing for real.
>
> You will now complete 15 rounds of the card choosing game, taking 10-15 minutes on average to complete.
>
> You will be able to take a short break between rounds, if you feel you need it.

> Round *N* out of 15
>
> Tap the button below to continue.  `[Continue]`

> Altogether on this round, you've collected/lost coins worth £X.XX.
>
> Tap the button below to continue.  `[Continue]`

When a round ends early:

> You've found the better card.
>
> You will skip the remaining turns and collect the remaining coins hidden under this card.
>
> Altogether on this round, you've collected coins worth £X.XX.

## 3. Vigour (piggy bank)

Interactive from the first screen — the participant taps a real piggy bank while
the instructions respond to what they do.

> Welcome to the piggy bank game!
>
> **Tap the piggy bank to shake it!**

> Tap the piggy bank to shake it!
>
> You can tap it again to keep on shaking...

> **Well done, You just got a coin out of the piggy bank!**
>
> You can always tap again for more coins. Try getting some more!

> Press Restart to try again, or Continue to proceed.  `[Restart]` `[Continue]`

> You will now play a few minutes of this game, collecting coins!
>
> Throughout the game, you will see different piggy banks with unique appearances:
>
> Vividness of piggy colors: Indicates how fast you need to shake it.
>
> Tail length: Longer piggy tails = more valuable coins.

> Types of coins you can win:
>
> 1 Penny
>
> 2 Pence
>
> 5 Pence
>
> Your bonus: At the end of the game, we will pay you a proportion of the total amount of coins collected across all the piggy banks.

> You will now play the piggy-bank game without a break for about four minutes.
>
> When you're ready, tap the piggy bank to begin.  `[Re-read instructions]`

## 4. Reversal (squirrels)

**1.**
> Let's start with the first game!
>
> Next, you will meet two friendly squirrels, each with a bag of coins to share. **Tap on either the left or right squirrel** to choose one. The squirrel you pick will give you a coin to add to your safe.

*Keyboard: "Use the arrow keys to choose either the left or right squirrel."*

**2.**
> One squirrel has higher-value coins, and the other has lower-value coins. But every few turns they secretly switch bags.
>
> Your goal is to figure out which squirrel has the better coins and collect as many high-value ones as possible.

**3.**
> You will now play the squirrel game for about five minutes without breaks.
>
> When you're ready, **tap either squirrel** to begin.

*Keyboard: "place your fingers comfortably on the left and right arrow keys… Press down both left and right arrow keys at the same time to begin."*

*Duration reads "three minutes" in the shorter 50-trial configuration.*

## Notes for reviewers

**Two wording problems, both keyboard-only.** Go/No-Go's keyboard variant reads
*"**To tap:** press the space bar"* and its practice says *"Tap this person's
picture"* — the label says tap, the action is a key. Same for the practice
screens. Invisible on the tablet the study runs on, but wrong if anyone runs it
on a desktop.

**Vigour says "tap" on desktop too.** Vigour was converted to pointer input
outright rather than keeping a keyboard path, so a desktop user clicks with a
mouse while being told to tap. Again only affects non-tablet use.

**Fullscreen prompts never appear.** Fullscreen enforcement is off, so any
"return to full screen" screen in the code is unreachable.

**Not covered here:** WM and the post-learning tests (which share PILT's
card-choosing instructions with different wording for the three-response
layout), Control, PIT, Delay Discounting, Max Press, Pavlovian Lottery, Open
Text and the acceptability ratings.
